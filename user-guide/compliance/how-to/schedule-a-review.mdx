A policy's review is one question: **Does the policy still hold, as written?** Answering it records the review, starts each linked standard's clock again, and removes the overdue item from the **Review Calendar**.

A review date is earned only by a recorded review. You cannot type one into the policy. Three acts record a review: answering the question, revising the policy's words, and confirming an imported policy's date from its manual.

## Before you start

- You need a role that can edit policies: institutional admin, compliance officer, department head, or someone an admin has granted policy editing by name. Anyone else sees the question with "An editor of policies answers this."

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the policy">
    Go to **Policy Inventory** and click the policy. If the review is due or a linked standard has changed, a **Review is due.** note appears above the tabs. It asks the question in place. You can also open the **Compliance** tab, where the question sits at the head of the policy's words.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the question">
    The question names the policy's standards, for example **Does the policy still hold, as written, for all three standards?** The line above it says when the review falls due and who recorded the last one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer Yes if nothing has changed">
    Click **Yes**. In **Yes · what you checked**, write what you read and against what, for example "Read the annual notice against 99.7; nothing in it has changed." Click **Send Yes**, then confirm.

    Every linked standard is confirmed today under your name, and each one's clock starts again. The words do not change. Your note goes on the policy's **History**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Answer No if something no longer holds">
    Click **No**. Each standard appears with two buttons:

    - **Still holds**: the policy still meets this standard.
    - **Flag**: the policy no longer meets it. Write what it no longer meets, then click **Flag**. A flag opens a compliance gap.

    Click **Enter review** to send your answers, or **Back to the question** to return with nothing sent.

    In the **Review is due.** note, **No** opens the **Compliance** tab on the standards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the result">
    A message confirms the review, for example "Review recorded, no change". The byline's **Reviewed** shows the day, your name, and how the review was recorded. The **Review Calendar** refreshes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Standards the policy answers in Certification Studio

A standard the policy answers only on Certification Studio's map keeps its own review too. When you answer **Still holds** or **Flag** for it, or answer **Yes**, the review is kept on each part of that standard the policy is confirmed to own, under your name. You can review such a standard on its own, without the policy's other standards. The policy's own review date moves only when you review all its standards at once.

In Certification Studio, choosing **Re-confirm** on a requirement whose wording changed also counts as that standard's review. A plain **Confirm** does not.

The **Review Calendar** reads such a standard's review as the oldest review among its parts. A part never reviewed reads the policy's own review date.

<Note>
If the policy has no linked standards, **No** means its words need to change. Revise them on the **Content** tab: the revision is its review. See [Update a policy](/user-guide/compliance/how-to/update-a-policy).
</Note>

## Start a review project

When a policy needs real work rather than a yes or no, start a compliance project to bring it up to date. Institutional admins and compliance officers can do this.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the dialog">
    In the policy's **Review is due.** note, click **Start a review project**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the proposal">
    The dialog shows the project it creates: **Review:** and the policy title, you as lead, a due date 30 days out, and the policy linked on the project's **Related** tab. Add an optional reason in **Why it needs review**. If your college asks new work to name a strategic objective, choose one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the approval route">
    **Approval route** says who decides the proposal and how quickly. The policy's owner is asked first, if it has one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit">
    Click **Submit for approval**. The dialog says who it is waiting on, with **Open in Approvals**. If your college's route asks no one, the review starts at once, with **Open the project**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

While the project is open, the Decay view in [Regulatory Intelligence](/user-guide/compliance/regulatory-intelligence) shows **Under review** beside the stale policy, with the project's name.

## What happens next

- Confirmed standards show a new **Current through** date.
- Flagged standards open compliance gaps that you work from the [compliance workspace](/user-guide/accreditation/compliance-workspace).
- The policy's **History** records the review with your name and, for a Yes, what you checked.
