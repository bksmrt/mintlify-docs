The Policy Inventory is the central register for every institutional policy your college or university maintains. Compliance officers and institutional admins use it to track policy status, review currency, rule coverage, and attached evidence.

## How to get here

Click **Policy Inventory** in the sidebar, under **Management**.

## What you see

### Toolbar

A search field sits at the top. Type to find policies by title or policy code. The filter icon at its right end opens **Views & filters**. After the search:

- The calendar button opens and closes the **Review Calendar** rail, on wide screens
- **Actions**: **Import** opens the document import dialog, and **Review calendar** opens the full [Review Calendar](/user-guide/compliance/review-calendar)
- **New Policy**: opens a dialog to enter a policy by hand

### Register list

The inventory opens as a journal register. Each row is one policy and shows:

- **Number**: position in the register
- **Policy**: the policy title, a short summary, its category or tags, and how many rules and documents it is linked to
- **Status**: Draft, Review, Approved, Published, or Archived
- **Last edited**: the most recent edit date

Pinned policies appear at the top of the list and are marked with a subtle stripe.

### Filters

Click the filter icon at the right end of the search field to open **Views & filters**. A line above the list says how many policies are showing. Save the search and filters you use often under **Saved views**.

- **Standing**: Drafts, In Review, Approved, Published, or Archived, each with a count. Choose one or more; none chosen shows them all.
- **Subject**: one or more categories, each with a count
- **Demo data**: a toggle that hides seeded demo policies so you only see real institutional records. The same setting applies on Projects and Curriculum.

### Required by your agencies

Above the register, a group lists the policies your confirmed agencies require. Each row names the policy, the standard that asks for it, and what the agency does without it (for example, "the agency penalizes without it"). Its standing is one of:

- **None yet**: you have no policy for it. Click **Start from the outline** to create a draft policy from the requirement, or **Open the draft** once one exists.
- **Not linked**: you have a matching policy that is not linked yet. Click **Link it**, and **Undo** if you change your mind.
- **Have it**: a linked policy covers it.

The group shows the three most critical rows. Click **View more** to see the rest.

### Imported policies waiting for confirmation

When an import has saved policies whose documents state a status or revision date, and nobody has confirmed it yet, a line appears over the register for anyone who may edit policies:

> 12 imported policies state a standing nobody has confirmed. Until then they are Drafts and count for nothing.

- Click **Review** to open the same standing review the import ends on.
- Click **Confirm** to accept what each document states. You confirm once more in a dialog. Each change is recorded on the policy's **History** in your name. Confirming does not approve a policy.

Each waiting row also says what its document stated, for example "The document says Approved, revised Jun 14, 2024", with its own **Confirm**.

### Academic policies

If your college has the Academic Operations module, the college's academic policies appear in their own group, by catalog year. Choose a year from the group's menu.

### Review Calendar rail (desktop)

On wide screens, a **Review Calendar** rail sits beside the register. It lists overdue and upcoming review deadlines and keeps them in view while you browse. A policy due because a standard it answers changed sits with the overdue items and reads **Review**. Fold the rail away with the calendar button in the toolbar, and click it again to bring it back. It opens on its own when it holds something new. A switch in the rail also shows the full list of policies required by your agencies.

## What you can do here

- **Open a policy**: click a row title to open the policy detail page
- **Create a policy**: click **New Policy**. Enter a policy code, category, title, summary, and content, then click **Create Policy**
- **Import policies**: from **Actions**, choose **Import**. See [Import policies from a manual](#import-policies-from-a-manual) below
- **Edit a policy**: from the row menu, choose **Edit**
- **View history**: from the row menu, choose **View History** to see every change and revision
- **Pin or unpin**: from the row menu, choose **Pin Policy** or **Unpin Policy** to keep key policies at the top of the list
- **Link documents**: from the row menu, choose **Link Documents** to attach evidence. A dialog opens with a searchable document list, suggested documents, and a scope selector (**Primary**, **Supporting**, or **Project Only**)
- **View in Certification Studio**: from the row menu, open the policy in Certification Studio
- **Archive**: from the row menu, choose **Archive**. The policy leaves the active inventory and stops counting as coverage, but its rule links are kept. Bringing it back restores its coverage exactly as it was
- **Unarchive**: under the **Archived** standing, choose **Unarchive** to restore a policy
- **Remove**: from the row menu, choose **Remove**. The policy leaves the active inventory and its rule links are marked removed, so any standard it was the only response to becomes a gap. Nothing is deleted: its versions, pins, and evidence are kept. The confirmation offers **Archive, keep its links** as the gentler choice

<Note>
Institutional admins, compliance officers, department heads, and anyone an admin has granted policy editing by name can create, edit, import, archive, and remove policies.
</Note>

## Import policies from a manual

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the files">
    From **Actions**, choose **Import**. Drag files into the dialog or click **Browse files**. Proof reads PDF, Word (`.docx`), Excel (`.xlsx`), CSV, plain text, and Markdown files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Analyze">
    Click **Analyze Document** (or **Analyze N Documents**). Proof reads each file for regulatory bodies, rules, policies, accreditors, and standards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what was found">
    Check and edit the extracted items. Click **Import** or **Dismiss** on any item. Then click **Import N Items**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm each policy's standing">
    The import ends on a review of the policies it saved. Each imported policy is saved as a Draft. Its row shows the status and last-revised date its document states.

    - If a document gives no status, you are asked whether it is the published edition. Answer **Yes** or **No**.
    - Edit any name, status, or date in place on its row.
    - Click **Confirm** to accept every row as shown, or close the dialog and confirm later from the inventory.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each file you import is also kept whole as a document on the [Documents](/user-guide/accreditation/documents) page. Each policy read from it names that document as its source.

<Note>
A confirmed policy leaves Draft and starts counting toward policy freshness from its confirmed last-revised date. Nothing is confirmed without a person.
</Note>

## Policy detail page

When you open a policy, you see a journal record. The byline above the title shows:

- The policy code and effective date
- **Reviewed**: the day of the last recorded review, who recorded it, and how (for example "no change", "revised", or "from the manual")
- **Owner**: the person or department that owns the policy
- **Review due**: the next review deadline, or "now — standard changed" when a linked standard has changed
- **Read from**: the document the policy was imported from, as a link
- The policy's standing, and **Archived** if it is archived

### Policy owner

Anyone who can read the policy sees its **Owner**, or **None**. If you can edit policies, click **Name one** (or **Change**) and search for a person or a department. The owner is saved at once, with **Undo** for a few seconds. A department is listed with its head.

The owner is who a review project for the policy asks first for approval.

### Review is due

When the review date has passed or a linked standard has changed, a **Review is due.** note appears above the tabs. It asks the review question and offers **Start a review project**. See [Complete a policy review](/user-guide/compliance/how-to/schedule-a-review).

### Tabs

- **Content**: the summary and the full policy text. Use **As of** to read the words that were in force on an earlier day, and **PDF of this reading** to save it.
- **Related**: the standards the policy is held to, each a link to its rule, with how current its review is. Below them, **Where used** lists the records that use the policy.
- **Compliance**: the policy's review question and the policy reading, where each marked sentence is read against its requirements. See [Collect policy readings](/user-guide/compliance/how-to/collect-policy-readings).
- **History**: every change and revision. Open a revision's row to compare it with the one before.

### Review Standing rail (desktop)

On wide screens, a side rail shows which linked standards need review, which are current, periodic review deadlines, and any linked standard sunset dates.

### The policy's assistant

When your college turns on the **Record assistant** on **Settings › Assistants**, the **p.** mark appears in the app's header on a policy's page, and the Review Standing rail folds into the assistant. Click the **p.** to open it:

- **Needs you** lists what needs you on this policy, for example **No owner is named.** with **Name one**, a linked standard that changed with **Open the standard**, or **This policy is due for review.** with **Review it**. Under it is the policy's review standing, for example "Review standing · 2 of 3 current".
- **Chat** answers questions about the policy from its own words. Each answer cites the passages it rests on; hover a marker to preview the passage, and the cited words are marked in the policy. An answer whose quote is not in the policy is not shown. Your conversation is kept, and **Your other conversations** lists your other threads.

With the **Record assistant** off, there is no **p.** and the page keeps its Review Standing rail.
