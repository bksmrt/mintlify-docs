SSO connections are managed in the **Single Sign-On** panel on the Proof operator console: the **SSO** tab of your institution's (or firm's) page. You set up a connection together with your Proof operator. You enter your identity provider's details yourself, in the WorkOS Admin Portal, and you publish the DNS records for your domains.

## Create a connection

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the SSO panel">
    Open the **SSO** tab for your institution or firm. The panel is titled **Single Sign-On**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a connection">
    Click **Add Connection**. In the **New SSO Connection** dialog, enter a **Display name**, such as "Okta" or "Azure AD".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a default role">
    Select the **Default role** for users who do not match any role mapping rule. It starts as `faculty`. The options are:

    - `institutional_admin`
    - `compliance_officer`
    - `department_head`
    - `faculty`
    - `auditor`
  </Step>
  <Step title="Require SSO (optional)">
    Check **Require SSO for users on matched domains (block fallback login)** to refuse standard sign-in for users on the connection's verified domains once it is active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the draft">
    Click **Create draft**. The connection starts in **draft** status.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Configure the IdP through WorkOS

Every new connection is WorkOS-backed, so you never paste IdP secrets into Proof. Instead, Proof generates a WorkOS Admin Portal link for the connection, and you enter your IdP details there:

- For **SAML** providers, you provide the IdP metadata URL or XML.
- For **OIDC** providers, you enter the issuer, client ID, and client secret.

WorkOS stores the secrets and handles the protocol handshake. Proof never sees the raw client secret or private key.

<Note>
  The connection's panel has no button for the Admin Portal today. Ask your Proof operator for the link. Generating it creates the connection's WorkOS organization, and sign-in cannot start until that organization exists.
</Note>

## Add and verify email domains

Before you can activate the connection, you must verify at least one email domain.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a domain">
    In the **Email Domains** section, type your domain (for example, `acme.edu`) and click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the TXT record">
    Proof shows a TXT record name and value. Add the TXT record to your DNS:

    ```text
    Name:  acme.edu
    Value: bksmrt-sso-verify=<token>
    ```

    You can also add it to `_bksmrt-sso.acme.edu` if you prefer. The value can sit inside a longer TXT record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Return to Proof and click **Verify**. Proof queries DNS at both names and, if the record is found, marks the domain as **verified**. If it is not found, you see **Verification failed** with the reason.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can add multiple domains to a single connection. Only verified domains are used for email lookup at sign-in.

## Map groups and claims to roles

In the **Role Mapping Rules** section, add a rule for each role you want to assign from your IdP:

- **Match**: `group`, `claim`, or `domain`.
- **Claim key**: for a `claim` rule, the claim to read.
- **Value**: the group name, claim value, or email domain to match.
- **Assign role**: one of the five roles above.
- **Pri**: the priority. Lower numbers are checked first; the default is `100`.

Click **Add** to save the rule.

See [SSO roles and sessions](/sso/roles-and-sessions) for how rules are evaluated.

## Test the connection

Before going live, click **Test** on the connection's panel. Proof runs a dry-run check that does not start a real sign-in flow.

For a WorkOS connection, the results show:

- **Checks**: pass or fail items, such as **WorkOS organization** and **WorkOS connection**.
- **Round-trip**: **Round-trip OK** when Proof reached the WorkOS API and found the connection, or **Round-trip failed** with the reason.

A failing test names the check that did not pass. "WorkOS organization not provisioned" means the Admin Portal link has not been generated yet.

## Activate or disable

Once you have at least one verified domain and the test passes, click **Activate**. The connection becomes **active** and users on verified domains are redirected to your IdP.

To pause SSO for the tenant, click **Disable**. Disabling a connection immediately revokes all active sessions tied to it, forcing users to re-authenticate.

To remove a connection permanently, click the delete (trash) button and confirm. Deleting also revokes all sessions tied to the connection.

## Force logout all

To end every active SSO session for the tenant at once, click **Force logout all** and confirm **Force logout**. This revokes sessions across every connection for the tenant. Users are signed out immediately and must re-authenticate on their next visit. Proof operators are not affected.

## Troubleshooting common errors

| Error | Cause | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Connection requires at least one verified email domain before activation" | You tried to activate before verifying a domain. | Add a domain, publish the TXT record, and verify it. |
| "Public mailbox domains (gmail.com, outlook.com, etc.) cannot be claimed" | You tried to add a consumer email domain. | Use your organization's domain, such as `university.edu`. |
| "Domain is already claimed by another connection" | Another connection has already verified this domain. | Contact that connection's admin, or use a different domain. |
| "Domain already added to this connection" | The domain is already on this connection. | Verify the existing entry instead. |
| "Invalid domain format" | The entered text is not a valid domain. | Enter only the domain, such as `acme.edu`. Proof strips a leading `@` or `https://` for you. |
| `TXT record "bksmrt-sso-verify=<token>" not found at …` | The TXT record is missing or has not propagated. | Wait for DNS propagation, then click **Verify** again. |
| "New SSO connections must use WorkOS" | Someone tried to create an in-house OIDC or SAML connection. | Create a WorkOS-backed connection. |
| "Could not start SSO sign-in" | Sign-in could not start, most often because the connection has no WorkOS organization yet. | Generate the Admin Portal link and finish the IdP setup there. |
| "Legacy SAML sign-in is no longer supported" or "In-house OIDC sign-in has been retired" | The connection was created with a retired in-house protocol. | Create a WorkOS-backed connection and move the domains to it. |
| "This email is already linked to a different organization's SSO" | The account is bound to another SSO connection. | Check which connection owns the user, then fix it there. |

<Note>
  A legacy SAML connection's panel still offers an **SP Metadata** download, but sign-in through legacy SAML connections is refused. New connections never need it: WorkOS provides the service provider details in the Admin Portal.
</Note>
